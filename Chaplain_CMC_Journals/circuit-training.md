Part 1: Weekly Training Schedule (Technician Qualification)
-----------------------------------------------------------

This seven-day rotation trains personnel to manage the critical balance between organic biology and electrical logic arrays.

```
       [ MON ]                 [ TUE ]                 [ WED ]
  Electrolyte Balance     Hydraulic Calibration   Insulation Integrity

         |                       |                       |
         +-----------------------+-----------------------+
                                 |
                               [ THU ] ---> Mid-Week System Diagnostics
                                 |
         +-----------------------+-----------------------+

         |                       |                       |
       [ FRI ]                 [ SAT ]                 [ SUN ]
   Parasite Mitigation     Terminal Overhaul      Emergency Response Run

```

-   Monday: Chemical and Electrolyte Balance

    -   Focus: Managing the ion carrier concentration in the core reservoir.
    -   Tasks: Measuring fluid density; mixing precise ratios of crushed copper sulfate and natron; testing reservoir conductivity.

-   Tuesday: Hydraulic and Pressure Calibration

    -   Focus: Regulating vascular flow without flooding the root system.
    -   Tasks: Operating gravity-fed sluice gates; clearing sediment blockages; adjusting irrigation intervals to maintain consistent transpiration.

-   Wednesday: Insulation and Boundary Integrity

    -   Focus: Preventing ground shorts along the main lines.
    -   Tasks: Inspecting bitumen (asphalt) linings; repairing cracked resin seals; pruning stray root fibers breaching the dielectric boundaries.

-   Thursday: Mid-Week System Diagnostics

    -   Focus: Testing end-to-end signal continuity.
    -   Tasks: Sending low-voltage test pulses from the central chapel node to peripheral outposts; logging response delays.

-   Friday: Parasite and Fungal Mitigation

    -   Focus: Eliminating biological resistance factors.
    -   Tasks: Applying antimicrobial vinegar washes; scraping mold layers off exposed vine trunks; isolating diseased plant segments.

-   Saturday: Terminal and Console Overhaul

    -   Focus: Maintaining human-to-network interfaces.
    -   Tasks: Polishing copper contact plates; realigning charcoal hardwood sliders; checking the mechanical leverage of ivory toggle switches.

-   Sunday: Comprehensive Emergency Response Run

    -   Focus: Simulating a sudden system-wide black start.
    -   Tasks: Simulating a severed line; deploying field teams to bridge broken paths using emergency copper-thread jumpers. [1]

Part 2: Troubleshooting Guide (Signal Drop Isolation)
-----------------------------------------------------

When a signal drops between the central chapel node and a territorial outpost, technicians must follow this step-by-step diagnostic matrix to isolate and resolve the failure. [2]

Phase 1: Localized Interface Testing
------------------------------------

-   Action: Test the console contact plates within the chapel using a known functional charge carrier.
-   If the local terminal registers 0: The issue is an interface failure. Clean the oxide buildup off the copper contact plates and tighten the copper wire thread wrapped around the root endings.
-   If the local terminal registers 1: The interface is clear. The fault lies downstream in the subterranean distribution network. Proceed to Phase 2.

Phase 2: Sectional Grid Segmentation
------------------------------------

-   Action: Access the nearest intermediate junction box or inspection trench along the affected route.

```
[ Central Chapel Node ] ======= (OK) ======= [ Junction A ] ------- (DROP) ------- [ Outpost ]
                                                  |
                                          (Check Point)

```

-   Test Point A: Check the signal potential at the halfway marker.
-   Analysis: If the signal is nominal at the junction but missing at the outpost, the fault is isolated to the outer half of the line. If the signal is already dead at the junction, the fault lies between the chapel and the junction.

Phase 3: Visual and Material Inspection
---------------------------------------

Once the failing sector is isolated, inspect the line for three primary failure modes:

1.  The Earth Ground Fault (Short Circuit):

    -   Sign: The root bundle has cracked through its bitumen casing and made contact with damp, raw soil.
    -   Fix: Sever the rogue root, dry the trench, and re-pour hot resin over the breach to restore insulation.

2.  The Vascular Blockage (Dry Line):

    -   Sign: The vine trunk is dry, brittle, or completely wilted, breaking the internal liquid filament.
    -   Fix: Immediately open the irrigation sluice gate for that sector and inject a high-concentration saline solution directly into the surrounding trench soil.

3.  The Parasitic Drain (Resistance Spike):

    -   Sign: Dense white mold or fungal rot has coated the exterior of the vine, sapping the voltage.
    -   Fix: Scrape the vine clean and coat the exterior in mineral oil to repel future fungal growth.
