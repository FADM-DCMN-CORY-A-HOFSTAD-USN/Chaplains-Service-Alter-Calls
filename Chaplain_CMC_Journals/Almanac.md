Developing a structural blueprint and operational protocol for a plant-based circuit node requires translating biological processes into clear engineering and logistical frameworks.

Here is the physical layout design and the logistical maintenance protocol for a central chapel node utilizing organic circuitry.

Part 1: Physical Layout Design & Structural Blueprint
-----------------------------------------------------

The physical architecture of the node must manage three primary functions: structural stability, fluid/electrolyte containment, and signal routing insulation.

1\. The Core Reservoir (Power & Electrolyte Source)
---------------------------------------------------

-   Location: Directly beneath or at the center of the structure's sanctuary floor.
-   Function: A deep cistern or natural spring packed with mineral salts (such as copper sulfate or natron) provides a constant pool of high-conductivity fluid.
-   Structure: Lined with non-porous stone or fired clay to prevent the electrolyte fluid from seeping into the surrounding foundations.

2\. The Root Distribution Conduit (The Bus Array)
-------------------------------------------------

-   Location: Subterranean trenches radiating outward from the central reservoir to the perimeter.
-   Function: Houses the thick taproots or trained vine networks that act as the main signal pathways.
-   Structure: The trenches are lined with natural bitumen (asphalt) or tree resin. This acts as a heavy dielectric insulator, preventing the low-voltage electrical signals traveling through the wet plant tissue from grounding out into the surrounding soil.

3\. The Terminal Interface Console (Logic Switching Station)
------------------------------------------------------------

-   Location: The main raised platform or altar area of the assembly space.
-   Function: Where human operators interact with the network to route signals or execute logic functions.
-   Structure: A frame of dense charcoal hardwood holds inset ivory slide-bars and beaten copper contact plates. Main root endings are bound to these copper terminals using tightly wrapped conductive copper thread, allowing physical manipulation to bridge or break specific circuits.

Part 2: Logistical Maintenance Protocol
---------------------------------------

Unlike metallic or synthetic circuitry, an organic, plant-based network requires constant active maintenance to prevent signal decay, biological rot, or environmental failure.

1\. Electrolyte Management and Chemical Balance
-----------------------------------------------

-   Salinity Regulation: The root channels must be periodically flushed with precise ratios of saline water to maintain internal ion concentration. If the soil becomes too fresh, conductivity drops; if it becomes too salty, the plant tissue dehydrates and dies.
-   Mineral Replenishment: Maintenance crews must regularly dump crushed copper ore or iron oxides into the central reservoir. The roots absorb these heavy metals, maintaining the metalloid density within the plant's internal vascular system (xylem and phloem).

2\. Hydration and Pressure Maintenance
--------------------------------------

-   Vascular Flow Control: The plants must be kept alive and actively transpiring. A complete lack of water stops the internal fluid movement, breaking the continuous liquid filament required to carry electrical potential across long distances.
-   Sluice Gate Operation: A system of gravity-fed water channels must be managed by the facility technicians to ensure the roots remain damp without becoming waterlogged, which would cause root rot and disintegrate the circuit path.

3\. Biological Pruning and Insulation Care
------------------------------------------

-   Parasite and Fungus Eradication: Mold or fungal growth on the vines acts as a parasitic resistor, draining current from the network. Regular cleaning using antimicrobial washes (like vinegar or specific plant oils) is required.
-   Root Guidance: Technicians must manually trim stray roots that attempt to breach the bitumen insulation layers. Any root that breaks through the resin boundary and touches raw earth creates a short circuit, disabling that entire sector of the territorial grid.
